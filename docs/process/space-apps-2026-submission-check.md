# NASA Space Apps 2026: submission and eligibility check

Checked against the [2026 participant terms](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/),
[project submission guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/)
and [participant FAQs](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/participant-faqs/).
This is a checklist, not a legal opinion or a determination of the team's eligibility.

## Items requiring the team's action

1. **Pre-event work:** The FAQ says teams are not allowed to start working on challenges before the hackathon.
   The repository has commits dated 13 November, before the 14 November event. Ask the Local Lead or global
   organizers whether and how this prior work may be submitted; do not assume eligibility or misstate the timeline.
2. **Submission:** Complete all required fields in English on the team Project page and click **Submit for
   Judging** by 15 November, 23:59 in the Local Event's time zone (the Team Owner's local time for the Universal
   Event). Check the Local Lead's judging deadline too. All team members must be confirmed at the same event;
   the FAQ says Universal Event teams need at least two participants for global judging.
3. **Demo and final project:** Provide public, login-free links to the final project and either a video of at most
   30 seconds or a slide deck of at most seven slides including title and introduction. The `/demo` page is only
   a placeholder and is not a submission-ready demo. The video must be in English or have English subtitles;
   slides must be in English. Test both links in a private browser.
4. **Sources and licences:** Cite every dataset, code, text, image and tool used in the project submission;
   identify the licences for non-NASA materials and verify their availability and redistribution terms.
   The guide requires at least one NASA dataset or resource for a Global Award. The repository has an Apache-2.0
   licence and source/asset notices, but the team must verify that the submission's references are complete.
5. **SI disclosure:** Describe where and how SI tools were used in the submission and in the project. The guide
   asks for indicators for SI-generated code/data in descriptive text and metadata, visible watermarks on
   SI-generated images/video, and disclosure in text and metadata for SI-generated audio. Verify which
   artefacts need indicators; do not label human-created or NASA material as generated without evidence.
   Generated content must not use, contain or modify NASA branding or design elements. The guide says SI
   should not be used to come up with the challenge solution idea.
6. **Commercial content:** The terms prohibit submitted material promoting commercial services or products.
   The site has a visible plans page and optional checkout links. Obtain an organizer's interpretation and
   remove or isolate any promotional content from the submitted project as appropriate; a prototype disclaimer
   alone does not establish compliance.
7. **Privacy and consent:** Exclude personally identifiable information and recognizable names, voices or
   likenesses of minors from the submission. Obtain required consent for adults featured in media, including
   written consent for individuals shown in submitted video where required by the media release.
8. **Affiliations and export controls:** The terms restrict participation in connection with Chinese or
   Chinese-owned entities, including universities; this is about affiliation, not nationality. The statement
   on `/about` does not itself establish team eligibility. Verify the actual facts and export-control
   obligations with the organizers. The cited terms do not state a corresponding blanket Russia-affiliation
   rule; do not infer one from the site's independent disclaimer.

The terms also grant NASA specified permissions to use submitted solutions and media; each participant should
read and accept the current terms personally. The [executive order on SI terminology](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/)
addresses the US executive branch; it does not itself impose terminology on this independent project.
