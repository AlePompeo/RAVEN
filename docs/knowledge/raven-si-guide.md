## RAVEN and RAVEN SI
RAVEN compares lunar-site energy and Earth communication opportunities. RAVEN engine is its calculation engine. RAVEN SI is the umbrella brand for a Learning Assistant, a general-purpose web assistant and an assistant for drafting Mission Planner reports. Available answers depend on the selected mode. Product explanations use cited material; report proposals require human review. There are no autonomous Internet browsing or web-search tools. RAVEN SI does not operate a spacecraft or certify a mission. Local document-only answers remain available without an online model.

## Mission Planner step by step
Choose mission objectives, sites or alternative periods, a time window and sampling interval, panel and radio assumptions. Run the simulation, complete the mission dossier, then review and export the report. The nine steps are Mission, Sites, Period, Energy, Reliability, Communications, Simulation, Mission dossier, Report. Reliability (optional study) has its own step after Energy: components, assumptions, A/B charts, thermal and radiation margins and statistics; results appear after the comparison is calculated. Learn provides lessons and quizzes. Explore provides technical visualizations.

## Energy and battery
Panel power is measured in W; generated or stored energy in Wh. Initial battery reserve can support operation in shadow. A full battery or zero generation can be a valid model result. Terrain can block sunlight even when the solar centre is above the local horizontal. Tracking is an idealized panel-orientation assumption. Battery minima and energy totals can cover only a known prefix: unknown later states must not be interpreted as zero.

## Radio, antennas and useful windows
RF link margin is measured in dB. Antenna gain, frequency, selected rate, geometry and terrain affect the link. A higher-gain antenna usually requires tighter pointing and appropriate hardware; a numerical gain is not qualified hardware selection. RAVEN engine includes terrain, diffraction, radio, power, battery and geometric ground-station visibility under declared assumptions. DSN visibility does not mean a scheduled station booking. Useful windows combine the model's operational-energy and communication conditions.

## Dossier, report and sustainability
The dossier distinguishes user declarations, limited-scope engine results, arithmetic on declared estimates and proposed work. Budget totals require complete attributed cost estimates; affordability is not economic feasibility. Mission CO2e requires declared phase emissions, sources and boundaries. Paper-production estimates are not a full lifecycle assessment or verified carbon savings. ESG includes Environmental, Social and Governance, not just carbon.

## RAVEN estimates in the dossier
After a calculation RAVEN engine proposes "RAVEN estimates" for fields the user did not fill: a risk register and contingencies (fixed rules over the engine results, each row citing the result field and value, severity from the share of the window affected, no probabilities), a requirements and verification matrix (user thresholds, minimum battery floor and the 3 dB margin against evaluated values: met, not met or unknown), the verification plan and the assembly, integration and test plan as templates over that matrix, the operational calendar of the evaluated window, and a power budget. Mass is estimated only if the user applies declared density and fraction defaults (tag I, editable) and is shown as a range; otherwise it stays unknown. An estimate is not a quote, design or forecast, never replaces a value the user typed, and is left out of totals and completion counts until the user accepts it. Accepted estimates appear in the appendix "RAVEN estimates you accepted" of the Expert report and PDF. RAVEN SI only writes or rewords text and is never the source of a number.

## Limits, exports and approvals
Unknown is not zero or impossible. The report follows a preliminary ECSS-inspired phase outline, not certified ECSS compliance. Real design, mass, launch analysis, hardware qualification, test records and authorizations need external evidence. Checkboxes select preview/PDF content; JSON retains the complete calculation and dossier. The RAVEN seal and DEMO blockchain certificate are not mission approval or a real blockchain transaction.

## Report modes: Learn and Expert
The Dossier and report have two modes. Learn (default for the Learning site mode) asks four essential questions (purpose, mission statement, success criteria and owner), shows the two thresholds already set in the Mission step, and allows one-click report generation; other dossier fields remain saved. Both report modes use the same order: executive summary, then ECSS-style phases 0 and A-F. The executive page gives the site comparison, trade-off decision and Moon map. The phases organize objectives and overlays; technical and economic feasibility; system definition and parameters; analytical models, formulas and validation limits; development and reliability; launch and operations; and end-of-life and sustainability. The report is English only, regardless of site language. Unknown values stay unknown, and the outline is preliminary planning structure, not an ECSS compliance claim.

Expert (default for the Advanced site mode) also includes selected appendices for reliability, parameters, the phase dossier, estimates and costs, and traceability. The "Customise sections" controls can hide optional appendices; the main executive summary and ECSS phases remain. You can switch mode in the Dossier or Report step without changing the site mode; the choice is saved with the draft. The numbers are identical in both modes. The completeness indicator counts accepted RAVEN estimates separately; proposed estimates do not count.

Mission class slider (Dossier, Learn and Expert): four named positions (small rover or payload, small commercial lander as default, medium lander, large or crew-class lander). It feeds RAVEN estimates of total cost band, development time, vehicle mass and launch class, launch CO2 (propellant burned times a combustion factor, a lower bound of CO2e) and launch and landing statistics, from public secondary sources. The report shows them automatically, labelled as estimates with range, assumptions and source, and totals on two lines: declared, and including RAVEN estimates. These are class scenarios, not an estimate of the user mission and not a quote; a value the user types always replaces an estimate, and each estimate can be dismissed. Quantities with no public source (manufacturing CO2e, reliability per component class, transfer success probability, cost of a small rover) stay unknown and are listed once in the checklist "Owner only, or no public source". The report has no legal section. Its risk list has at most five rows merged across A and B.
